## Operational Directives
- Raise visual and radio surveillance when approaching the Strait of Hormuz and the Arabian Gulf.
- Plan additional transit time and expect high traffic density and blockade-enforcement operations.
- Do not transit the Strait of Hormuz or the Gulf of Oman without a clear operational need, and document the transit justification.
- Expect questioning and inspection if operating as a neutral or empty vessel, and prepare cargo and destination documents.
- Record any call requesting a change of route as a security incident, and identify the calling party and time.
- Keep the vessel’s AIS and VHF communications operational, and report any interruption immediately.
- In the Gulf of Aden and the Red Sea, monitor piracy warnings and strengthen self-protection measures.
- Distinguish official reports from professional reporting before deciding to change route.
- Do not attribute any incident to an executing entity in the vessel log before independent confirmation or an official statement.

## Situation Reading and Indicator Analysis
During the period, the maritime situation moved from a professional warning to an official product. A blockade-enforcement advisory was issued. Avoiding unnecessary transit became an announced recommendation. This means that the restriction on transit became explicit rather than implicit.

The Iranian threat to respond to vessel escorts raises the risk of friction along the transit route. However, media reporting does not establish a new field measure. At the same time, discussion of a seven-day plan and a proposal under review recurred. This is a negotiating track that has not become a navigational outcome.

In the Gulf of Aden and Yemen, risks are shifting from vessels to civilian infrastructure. The targeting of Aden Airport and the Ministry of Health warning affect the access of aid and crews, not commercial transit alone.
